Post-mortems

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 1

Missed: 9,190 people when stopped in 2022. Homes were too far from jobs, so commutes failed.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 2

Missed: peaked at 35,690 people in 2011, then traffic, power wiring and debt; stopped in 2017.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 3

Missed: destroyed by an earthquake and fire in 2011 at 18,410 people.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 4

Missed: stalled at about 30,000 people in 2021; low land value and ten loans.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 5

Missed: stuck near 6,700 people in 2012; the road link to the neighbors was missing for 10 years.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 6

Missed: stuck near 11,500 people in 2013. Every check passed, but three loans cost more than the city earned in tax.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 7

Missed: no loans and no debt, but only 3,850 people by 2010. Growing from savings alone was far too slow.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 8

Missed: stalled near 8,000 people in 2014. Every check passed, but dense-only zoning grew slowly and the second loan did not pay back.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 9

Missed: stuck near 7,500 people by 2022. Demand was at maximum for years but there was no money for more land; a power plant burned down with no fire station.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 10

Missed: stalled near 14,000 people by 2018. The early blocks were smaller and later than the plan, then five loans cost more than the city paid in tax.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 11

Missed: 20,790 people by 2020, the second-best start. The seaport worked but came 9 years late; factory land fell behind; seven loans cost more than the city paid in tax.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 12

Missed: the fastest start of all (12,030 by 2004), then flat at about 21,000 from 2012. Shops were capped without an airport, unemployment hit 19%, and each person cost about $2.90 to house - too much to reach 100,000.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 13

Missed: the best city at year 6 (17,790), peak 20,070 in 2008. An automatic tax rule pushed factory tax to 12%, industry collapsed and unemployment reached 26%.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 14

Missed: 23,580 people by 2013. Low taxes kept the city healthy (unemployment 4-7%), but growth was slow and nine loans cost more than the city paid in tax.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 15

Missed: 18,260 people by 2011. Dense homes worked (27 people per tile instead of 18), but factories stopped growing even after a second seaport, so jobs capped the city.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 16

Missed: 24,870 people by 2014. Putting factories next to homes fixed the 18,000 stall, but nine loans and too few jobs for the dense homes stopped the city.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 17

Missed: peak 29,090 people in 2013, the best city since attempt 2 and the fastest start of all. Eleven loans and a yearly deficit slowed it to about 1,000 people a year.

Claudeville 3 · Attempt 18

Missed: peak 28,920 people in 2015. Attempt 17 replayed with extra income reached the same plateau: too many homes for the jobs, and each new district needed a loan.
