How fast problems got spotted

Both cities hit the same kinds of problems. The difference was how long each one went unnoticed. In attempt 2, Claude read the city's data from the save file every game year: water coverage, power coverage, abandoned buildings and jammed roads.

What changed

  • Day 1

    The basics went in before anything grew

    10 water pumps, a road grid, a coal plant and a neighbor link on the first day. In attempt 1 each of these arrived years late, after it had already done damage.

  • Cash

    Money was spent early

    Attempt 2 ran its cash down to about $5,000 by 2004 and kept it there. Attempt 1 sat on $10,000–$16,000 for 20 years.

  • Tax 5%

    Low tax from the start

    5% for the whole game. Attempt 1 stayed at 7% until 2025, even though tax only brought in about $300 a year.

  • Demand

    Built what the city asked for

    When homes were in demand Claude added a north district by the lake (2003). When jobs ran short it added factory land (2005, 2013). Attempt 1 mostly kept to its first layout.

  • Checks

    Trusted the save file over the screenshot

    Every build was checked against the saved game, so clicks that landed on the wrong tile and roads that hit water were caught within minutes.

Same in both

  • Jobs and homes kept swinging. Attempt 2 still dipped in 2006, 2008 and 2013, but each dip lasted about a year instead of a decade.
  • Clumsy controls. Wrong tiles, accidental scrolling and surprise pop-ups happened in both runs. In attempt 2 the checks caught them quickly.
  • Zero loans. The no-borrowing rule held in both runs.

Where attempt 2 was weak

  • No public services. No police, schools or hospitals all game. Costs stayed near zero, which made the budget goal easy, and life expectancy stayed around 59.
  • Abandonment never cleared. 40–57 abandoned buildings at any time from 2014 on.
  • Traffic was rising by 2018 and would have been the next problem.
  • How "profitable" was measured. Cash up from January to January with building frozen. The game's own estimate agreed: +$363 for 2015.

Attempt 1 didn't fail because the game was hard. It failed because Claude only looked at the city's data once something broke. Attempt 2 checked every year, so each problem cost about a year instead of a decade.

Both runs were played by Claude Code in a cloud container, controlling SimCity 2000 (DOS) through DOSBox and reading population, cash and city health straight from the game's save file. Live dashboards: attempt 1 · attempt 2.
