# Expert strategies for 100,000+ population in SimCity 2000 (classic, 1993)

Scope note: sources found are mostly fan pages (patcoston.com, ibiblio GameBytes "Zen and the Art of SimCity 2000", somacon.com, ludo.guide, dosgameclub forum, GOG forum). Reddit, simcity.fandom.com and Giant Bomb could not be fetched (blocked / 402 / 403), so claims from them appear only as search-snippet summaries and are marked UNVERIFIED. No source with a year-by-year population timeline for a 100k city was found.

## Q1. What experienced players do in years 1-5, 5-10, 10-20 (build order, zoning amount, borrowing, taxes)

### Takeaway
No source gives a strict year-by-year opening. The consistent advice: power plant + roads + a small balanced R/C/I mix first, zone in small increments and let the city stabilise, keep taxes about 7-8% for growth (9% noticeably depresses demand), borrow at most one bond at a time and only if profit covers the interest, and add services only when sims ask for them or grades drop.

### Cited Findings
- Every city needs a power plant, a grid of roads (not rails), and a mix of zones within 3 tiles of a road. — [somacon.com tips](https://www.somacon.com/p606.php)
- Zones only develop within 3 squares of a road; rail or subway also works within 3 squares "but growth is much slower". — [Zen and the Art of SimCity 2000](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- "Start zoning in small increments", a few residential and industrial zones at a time, let the city stabilise; big zoning booms cause abandonment and unemployment. Commercial demand is small in low-population cities: zoning 20 commercial squares may leave only about 8 developed. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- Taxes: around 9% noticeably depresses demand; 7-8% gives better growth demand. Author used 9% only to restore revenue. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- Ludo.guide (summarising a Nov 2024 r/SimCity testing write-up): at 9% property tax "strict adherence" to the desired RCI ratio is needed; lower rates allow more deviation; "higher taxes (especially above 9%) can make things really tricky" and cause unstable population swings. — [ludo.guide help](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/simcity-2000/help); [ludo.guide help-2](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/simcity-2000/help-2)
- Contrary view: one forum player keeps taxes "relatively high at 10% or so" while demand is strong in all zones; another prefers lower taxes and saving gradually to fund power plant replacements. — [DOS Game Club forum](https://www.dosgameclub.com/forums/topic/my-experience-with-sc2k/)
- "Sims can get grumpy if taxes are too high (generally above 10-12%)." — [ludo.guide population](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/simcity-2000/population)
- Tax changes take "at least a few months" to fully affect the RCI demand bars; demand bars already include the tax rate. — [GOG forum](https://www.gog.com/forum/simcity_series/sim_city_2000_rci_demands)
- Lower taxes once you can afford it to encourage growth. — [patcoston advice](https://patcoston.com/co/advice.aspx)
- Bonds: $10,000 increments; yearly interest = rate x $100 per bond (7% = $700/yr). Borrow only if profit covers the interest and you can repay the principal; refinance if rates fall (7% -> 4% saves ~$300/yr). — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- "Taking out more than one bond at a time is not recommended ... It would be impossible to pay off the interest." — [patcoston Q&A](https://patcoston.com/co/QA.aspx)
- Forum veteran: "loans rarely pay off"; recessions (interrupted revenue, austerity driving residents away) are the main brake on growth. Build at a steady pace rather than reacting to demand swings. — [DOS Game Club](https://www.dosgameclub.com/forums/topic/my-experience-with-sc2k/)
- Early services: fund new schools/hospitals at ~25% in year one; you can often keep an "A" grade at ~25%; raise funding when grades drop and build another only when at 100% and still dropping. Keep about $1,000 treasury for emergencies; let the city run between expansion phases. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- Convert light zones to dense to grow the tax base without new roads, pipes, or power lines. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- Ordinances that generate income: income tax, sales tax, legalized gambling, parking fines. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)

### Inferences
- For a $10,000 Medium start with loans allowed: one bond early (to fund the first block) is defensible, but more than one simultaneous bond is consistently warned against.
- Run tax at 7-8% during growth phases; 9%+ only when cash is needed and RCI ratio is well balanced.

### Gaps
- No source gives a concrete "year 1 / year 5 / year 10" build list with money amounts.
- The original Nov 2024 r/SimCity post (id 1h3kjko) could not be fetched; only ludo.guide's summary of it was readable.

## Q3. Layout patterns that maximise population

### Takeaway
Dense zones in road-efficient stripes or 6x6 blocks, R/C/I mixed close together (within 10-15 tiles), roads for small cities and rail/subway for big ones, highways from ~50k. The single biggest growth driver (per recent testing) is keeping the game's desired RCI zone ratio, which shifts with population.

### Cited Findings
- Building capacity: small buildings 10 people per tile; medium 20/tile at low density (80 per building) or 30/tile high density (120 per building); large 40/tile (360 per building). — [somacon](https://www.somacon.com/p606.php)
- A 6x6 zone block with roads between blocks fits 9 medium (2x2) or 4 large (3x3) buildings with no waste. A 3x3 zone needs road on at least 3 tiles of one side and 1 tile of another to grow into a large building. — [somacon](https://www.somacon.com/p606.php)
- A zone needs access to other zone types within about 10-15 tiles; undeveloped zoning should have a road within 3 tiles. — [somacon](https://www.somacon.com/p606.php)
- Stripe grid: two roads with six rows of zoning between them; very road-efficient, good for dense zoning, no room for public buildings. Block grid: zones around a central hole that holds schools/hospitals/police/fire/parks (these need no road access); extra roads add smog. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- "Adhering to the game's desired RCI zone ratio is the primary driver of population growth"; the ratio shifts as population grows; only RCI zones need transit; each zone type needs a valid path to the other two; "recreation, ports, and neighbor connections matter most for large population gains"; high land value (water, scenery, no rubble/crime/pollution) supports dense development; essentials are only power, RCI zones and transit. — [ludo.guide help](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/simcity-2000/help)
- RCI ratio by city size (page text appears mistyped; the plausible reading is): small city 4:1:3 heavy industry, 10k-60k 4:2:2, over 60k 4:3:1 heavy commercial; rule of thumb R = C + I. — [somacon](https://www.somacon.com/p606.php) (thresholds on the page conflict; reading is an inference)
- Other ratio advice: 1/2 R, 1/4 I, 1/4 C — [patcoston advice](https://patcoston.com/co/advice.aspx); ~50% R, 30% I, 20% C — [DOS Game Club](https://www.dosgameclub.com/forums/topic/my-experience-with-sc2k/)
- Dense zoning = more population and more pollution; light = less of both. Build highways when population exceeds 50,000; a highway needs an onramp. Use bus/subway/rail to reduce pollution. — [patcoston advice](https://patcoston.com/co/advice.aspx)
- "Effective road layout and the implementation of subways and railways are make-or-break" for big-city traffic. (ludo.guide lists 3x3, 4x4, 6x6, 9x9 and 6x13 grid patterns but their content was not retrievable.) — [ludo.guide big cities](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/simcity-2000/transportation-for-big-cities)
- "Sea and rail affect industry demand, road and airport affect commerce demand"; build road and rail links to neighbour cities, a seaport and later airports. Roads need property along them up to the map edge to create travel incentive. — [DOS Game Club](https://www.dosgameclub.com/forums/topic/my-experience-with-sc2k/)
- Bus depot can carry up to half of commuters if well placed. — [patcoston OBJDEF](https://patcoston.com/co/OBJDEF.aspx)
- Subway available 1910, buses 1920, highways 1930. — [patcoston Q&A](https://patcoston.com/co/QA.aspx)

### Inferences
- For a 100k target: roughly 100k / 30 per tile (dense medium) = ~3,300 developed residential tiles at full density, or ~2,500 at 40/tile large buildings; plus jobs zones. Dense zoning and high land value (parks, water) are needed to reach large buildings.
- Ratio must shift towards commercial above ~60k; adding only industry late will stall growth.

### Gaps
- The "no roads, only rail" and "subway everywhere" tricks: no source found confirming or quantifying them for SC2K. Zen says rail/subway-only zones grow "much slower".
- Exact desired-ratio formula not found.

## Q4. When/why to add police, fire, schools, colleges, hospitals, stadium, seaport, airport, marina, zoo

### Takeaway
Experienced players add services reactively (when sims request them or grades drop). Seaport (~10k) and airport (~15k) are the key demand unlockers for industry and commerce; recent testing says health/education matter little for growth.

### Cited Findings
- "Don't add services until Sims request them" is the best rule for a large city; add schools/hospitals when at maximum capacity and grade drops below A; wait for requests for stadiums, marinas, big parks. — [patcoston STRAT1](https://patcoston.com/co/STRAT1.aspx)
- Seaport "needed around 10,000 population"; airport "needed around 15,000 population". — [patcoston OBJDEF](https://patcoston.com/co/OBJDEF.aspx)
- Build an airport to attract more sims; keep tall buildings away from it to avoid crashes. Schools near residential reduce crime; many parks in high-crime areas; few fire stations needed only if disasters are off. — [patcoston advice](https://patcoston.com/co/advice.aspx)
- With disasters on, build more fire stations since each adds an emergency unit. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- Health and education "have minimal impact on overall progression". — [ludo.guide help](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/simcity-2000/help)
- Police near commercial, fire stations near industry, parks/schools near housing; keep fire funding at 100%. — [DOS Game Club](https://www.dosgameclub.com/forums/topic/my-experience-with-sc2k/)
- Reward thresholds: Mayor's House 2,000; City Hall 10,000; Statue 30,000; Military Base 60,000; Llama Dome 90,000; Arcologies 120,000. — [patcoston Q&A](https://patcoston.com/co/QA.aspx)

### Inferences
- Plan coastline/river access for a seaport before ~10k and a 2x6+ flat area for an airport before ~15k.

### Gaps
- SC2K demand caps (residential cap lifted by stadium, commercial by airport, industrial by seaport) are widely remembered by players but NO SC2K source confirming exact caps was found; numbers found (27,000 residential cap without relief) are for SimCity 3000 — [somacon p610 / SC3000](https://somacon.com/p610.php). UNVERIFIED for SC2K.

## Q5. Power and water choices by year

### Takeaway
Coal (200 MW, $4,000) is the classic early plant; gas is cheap to place but only 50 MW; nuclear (500 MW, $15,000) from 1955; microwave (1,600 MW) from 2020; fusion (2,500 MW, $40,000) from 2050. Water is optional but essential for dense growth.

### Cited Findings
- Years: 1900 coal, hydro, oil; 1950 gas; 1955 nuclear; 1980 wind; 1990 solar; 2020 microwave; 2050 fusion. — [patcoston Q&A](https://patcoston.com/co/QA.aspx)
- Cost / MW output: coal $4,000/200; hydro $400/20; oil $6,600/220; gas $2,000/50; nuclear $15,000/500; wind $100/4; solar $1,300/50; microwave $28,000/1,600; fusion $40,000/2,500. Cost per MW: coal $20, hydro $20, oil $30, gas $40, nuclear $30, wind $25, solar $26, microwave $17.5, fusion $16. — [patcoston Q&A](https://patcoston.com/co/QA.aspx)
- Don't rely on solar, hydro or wind as main power; build fusion when affordable and available. — [patcoston advice](https://patcoston.com/co/advice.aspx)
- Hydro/wind need no rebuild after 50 years; other plants must be replaced (coal costs twice over a century). — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)
- Water treatment plant 1935; desalination 1990. — [patcoston Q&A](https://patcoston.com/co/QA.aspx)
- Water "not required, but essential for a dense population". — [patcoston advice](https://patcoston.com/co/advice.aspx)
- Pump next to fresh water gives ~2x the output of one on dry land; desal costs $1,000 (~4x a river pump) but ten $100 pumps give more; water towers store 40,000 gallons; water shortages can cause abandonment. — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html); [GOG forum](https://www.gog.com/forum/simcity_series/sim_city_2000_rci_demands)
- Pumps need power; output varies with rainfall. — [patcoston OBJDEF](https://patcoston.com/co/OBJDEF.aspx)

### Inferences
- For 2000-2050 (game start 2000): nuclear (500 MW) is the cheapest big step before 2020 but has meltdown risk with disasters on; microwave from 2020 is the best $/MW until fusion in 2050. Rough need: one player says ~60k needs ~2 plants (coal-scale).
- On a river map, pumps placed along the river give the 2x bonus; this is the cheapest water.

### Gaps
- Microwave plant failure risk (mis-targeted beam) not quantified.

## Q6. Common mistakes that cap cities at 20-40k and how players break through

### Takeaway
Caps come from RCI-ratio imbalance (especially too little commercial later), high taxes, recessions from over-borrowing, no neighbour/sea/air connections, pollution/crime lowering land value, water/power shortages, and traffic in road-only cities.

### Cited Findings
- Ratio adherence is the main driver; taxes set tolerance; ports, recreation and neighbour connections matter most for large gains. — [ludo.guide help](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/simcity-2000/help)
- Stagnation fixes suggested: more jobs (I/C zones), check power capacity, road connections, police coverage, water storage. — [GOG forum](https://www.gog.com/forum/simcity_series/sim_city_2000_rci_demands)
- Recessions from loans/austerity and power plant failure, earthquakes and fires end cities. — [DOS Game Club](https://www.dosgameclub.com/forums/topic/my-experience-with-sc2k/)
- Bankruptcy recovery: raise taxes gradually, cancel ordinances except energy conservation, cut school/hospital funding, but keep transport funded (unfunded roads/rail decay fast and hurt commerce). — [Zen](http://www.ibiblio.org/GameBytes/issue18/misc/zen.html)

### Inferences
- A city stalling around 25-30k with C demand strongly positive likely lacks commercial zoning, an airport, or road neighbour links; I demand stalling suggests a seaport/rail link is needed.

### Gaps
- No primary source quantifies a hard cap at 20-40k in SC2K.

## Q7. Arcologies

### Takeaway
Arcologies unlock at 120,000 population (so they cannot help reach 100k). Plymouth (from 2000) is the only one available before 2050.

### Cited Findings
- Plymouth 55,000 residents, $100,000, available 2000; Forest 30,000, $120,000, 2050; Darco 45,000, $150,000, 2100; Launch 65,000, $200,000, 2150; arcologies unlock at 120,000 population. — [patcoston Q&A](https://patcoston.com/co/QA.aspx)
- Launch capacity given as 60,000 elsewhere (Giant Bomb, search snippet; UNVERIFIED). Plymouth also listed as 50,000 by one guide (UNVERIFIED). — [Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-13760/)
- Arcologies generate a lot of crime; they are meant to be self-contained. — [patcoston STRAT1](https://patcoston.com/co/STRAT1.aspx)
- Early DOS versions: an updater was needed to let all arcologies count toward population. — [Icon Bar FAQ mirror](https://www.iconbar.com/Sim_City_2000_FAQ/news1022.html) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- For "100k by 2050", arcologies are irrelevant: they appear only after 120k.

### Gaps
- None of the fetched sources confirm whether the 120k threshold is checked on the current population.
